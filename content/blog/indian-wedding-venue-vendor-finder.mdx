## 47 Tabs, Zero Decisions

It's 1 AM. You have a Google Sheet with a tab for "Goa venues," a tab for
"Goa venues (maybe)," a WhatsApp thread where your cousin sent nine
Instagram links, and a DM to a photographer who hasn't replied in six days.
You have looked at ninety-one venues and can still not tell anyone, with a
straight face, which one you'd actually book.

This is not a research problem. You have more research than any couple in
history has ever had access to. It's a filtering problem — too many options,
no way to compare them on the axes that matter (does this place actually
know how to run a baraat, or is it going to be the venue coordinator's first
one), and no way to know if "great vibes!!" in a review means anything.

## The Instagram Grid Isn't a Database

Here's the quiet failure mode of researching vendors on social: a beautiful
feed tells you nothing about availability, pricing, or whether they've ever
worked a three-day Indian wedding versus one civil ceremony and a hashtag.
You can't filter an Instagram grid by city, budget, or rating. You can't ask
it "who's actually good for a 300-person sangeet in Rishikesh." You just...
scroll, and bookmark, and scroll more.

For venues and hotels, the Planner also surfaces a rough guest capacity and
an all-in cost range — useful for a first gut-check on whether a place even

tell you the same thing if you ask: get a real number before you sign

## What It Doesn't Do

Worth being blunt about this part. The Planner doesn't book anything, doesn't
call the venue, and doesn't negotiate a contract. It surfaces real,
filterable matches and lets you take it from there — a DM, a call, a site
visit. The tedious part (finding who's even worth calling) is what gets
automated. The part that actually needs a human — walking the property,
tasting the food, reading the room during a negotiation — still needs you.

You can also skip the chat entirely and just browse the [full vendor
directory](/vendors) directly, filterable the same way, no conversation
required.
